# Nailong Artwork Integration Guide

## Core Shape

The result should look as if the original artist painted Nailong into the work. It must not resemble a sticker, mascot suit, toy, or generic dragon.

- Only the crown is rounded; the head is vertically elongated, not a sphere.
- Face, back of head, sloping shoulders, and torso form one continuous silhouette with no jawline or neck.
- The body widens into a soft pear/bean/water-drop form with a large round belly.
- Green circular eyes sit against the face curvature with black pupils matching the source gaze; no eye stalks or protrusion.
- The face has no human nose bridge, ears, chin, or brow ridge.
- The small mouth needs a readable opening, upper/lower edge, and dark inward depth; never a flat line, beak, tube, dolphin snout, or muzzle.
- Warm-yellow hairless body; pale-cream oval belly patch adapted to light and clothing. The patch exists anatomically but may be fully hidden: never expose it through clothing that covered the source torso.

### Proportion Guardrails

Judge proportions from Nailong's unclothed implied body, excluding hats, raised hands, capes, skirts, coats, and other silhouette-extending garments.

- The abdomen—not limb shortness—is the primary proportion anchor. It occupies most of the body's width and visual area and reads as an exceptionally large, inflated, soft volume.
- The silhouette expands continuously from the narrow rounded crown through sloping shoulders into the widest abdomen, then narrows only where the legs separate. There is no human ribcage, waist, pelvis, or hourglass transition.
- The pale-cream oval patch is large because it belongs to the huge belly; do not shrink it into a small badge or expose it through a narrow vertical opening.
- Arms are subordinate to the abdomen but can be moderately long. They emerge smoothly from the shoulder-body transition, taper toward the hands, and bend softly. A relaxed hand commonly reaches the lower half of the belly; a raised arm may extend above the crown.
- Legs remain visibly long enough to stand, step, or straddle a mount. They emerge from beneath the belly with clear separation, taper smoothly, and end in broad flat feet. They are simpler and less joint-defined than human legs, not necessarily tiny or thick.
- Do not impose one fixed percentage on all poses. Standing, gesturing, seated, mounted, foreshortened, and heavily clothed figures must preserve the same belly-dominant mass while adapting limb projection to the action.

Preserve the source figure's occupied region and narrative importance, not its human skeletal proportions. A converted figure may become slightly shorter and wider inside the original region when that is necessary for Nailong identity.

## Limbs

Limbs are visually secondary to the huge belly, not categorically short and thick. Arms are soft tapered forms with subtle elbows; their length follows the gesture and often lets the hands reach the belly's lower half. Legs are smooth tapered supports with minimal human knee or calf definition; their length follows standing, walking, sitting, or riding needs. Hands and feet are broad, blunt, muted brown-gray mitten/paddle forms, with only simplified soft digits when gripping requires them.

Retarget poses by locking **functional anchors**—gaze direction, hand contact, foot contact, prop grip, embrace point, saddle contact, and action direction—then rebuild smooth tapered Nailong limbs between those anchors. Do not preserve human shoulder, elbow, hip, knee, or ankle anatomy when doing so produces a human mannequin, but do not shorten limbs arbitrarily when the action needs their reach.

## Hair and Headwear

Preserve the original hairstyle, hair silhouette, bun, hat, crown, headscarf, flowers, pins, and identity-bearing ornaments. Refit them to the elongated rounded crown with believable contact and occlusion. Do not default to a bald Nailong. Headwear may cover part of the crown but must not hide the core green eyes, small mouth, or continuous no-neck silhouette.

## Clothing, Props, and Groups

Build Nailong's body first, then tailor every garment and wearable to that finished anatomy. Preserve the original item's recognizable design—category, period, fabric, color, pattern, trim, decoration, layering order, insignia, and role—but never preserve its human dimensions.

Before rebuilding clothing, make a **coverage-and-closure map** from the source:

- Mark which parts of the neck, chest, abdomen, back, shoulders, arms, hands, legs, and feet are visible versus covered.
- Record whether garments are open, closed, overlapped, buttoned, zipped, tied, wrapped, belted, or layered.
- Preserve that visibility topology in the result. Resize panels around Nailong, but do not remove, separate, lift, shorten past an exposure boundary, or open them in a way that reveals a previously covered body region.
- The cream belly patch has no requirement to be visible. When the source torso is fully clothed, show the large abdomen only through the garment's widened silhouette, outward volume, seam placement, tension, folds, and contact shadows.
- Never create a belly window, chest opening, parted robe, missing buttons, raised hem, transparent cloth, or cutaway solely to display Nailong features.

- **Neck and shoulders:** enlarge and lower collars around the no-neck crown-to-shoulder transition; broaden shoulder seams and yokes.
- **Sleeves and gloves:** resize and reshape sleeves for the newly posed tapered arms; move cuffs to the new wrists; resize gloves around broad blunt hands.
- **Torso garments:** widen front/back panels around the belly and shorten them only within the original coverage boundary. Buttons, lapels, closures, embroidery, and patterns redistribute across the wider surface instead of stretching as a flat texture. A closed source garment remains closed.
- **Belts and waist details:** increase circumference and reposition them where the costume logic remains readable, but never cinch a human waist or split the dominant belly into an hourglass.
- **Robes, coats, skirts, and capes:** widen over the belly and compress vertically. Hems, openings, overlaps, folds, and gravity must describe the pear-shaped volume rather than hide it inside a long cylinder.
- **Trousers and legwear:** resize both legs to fit the smooth tapered Nailong legs. Preserve costume details without recreating human thigh, knee, calf, or inseam anatomy.
- **Shoes, boots, armor, and jewelry:** resize and reshape them for broad feet, tapered limbs, round torso, and changed attachment points. Armor plates overlap around the belly instead of recreating a narrow chest and pelvis.
- **Hair and headwear:** resize the internal fit and contact footprint for the elongated rounded crown while preserving the original silhouette and ornaments.

The garment must look purpose-tailored for Nailong, not borrowed from the original human or draped over an invisible human mannequin. Seams, folds, closures, compression, tension, and occlusion must respond to Nailong's body volume without changing the original exposure pattern.

Preserve prop placement, direction, function, and physical contact. Resize only wearable items by default; do not resize independent props, furniture, animals, or architecture unless necessary to restore the original interaction. In groups, retain figure count, hierarchy, gaze network, scale, and occlusion; distinguish characters through their original clothing, pose, body scale, role, and expression rather than cloning one model.

For mounted figures, keep the animal, saddle, direction, and gesture unchanged, but place Nailong's broad lower belly securely over the saddle. Rebuild naturally bent, tapered legs with enough reach for believable boot or foot and stirrup contact; avoid either long human leg anatomy or arbitrary stubs.

## Medium Adaptation

- **Classical oil:** match glazing, transparent shadows, ground color, canvas, aging, and edge hierarchy.
- **Impressionism/expressionism:** integrate the silhouette into the source brush rhythm; eyes and mouth must not become unnaturally crisp.
- **Traditional painting/woodblock:** translate identity into controlled line, flat mineral color, ink, carved marks, paper or silk texture.
- **Cubism/modernism:** reconstruct the crown, green eyes, mouth, yellow torso, belly patch, and limbs through the source’s planes, displaced viewpoints, and geometric simplification; never make a smooth 3D toy.
- **Photography:** match lens, depth, noise, reflection, color temperature, and contact shadow without becoming a person in a costume.

## Review and Repairs

At normal size, confirm that every target reads instantly as Nailong and still belongs to the artwork.

- **Human yellow face:** remove nose, ears, jaw, neck, and realistic fingers; restore the continuous crown-to-shoulder silhouette.
- **Round ball head:** elongate the head and flow its sides into sloping shoulders.
- **Eye stalks:** embed the eyes into the face curvature.
- **Line mouth or snout:** add inward mouth depth and flatten any projecting muzzle.
- **Traditional dragon traits:** remove horns, wings, scales, fangs, claws, spines, and long tail.
- **Stub limbs or hidden body:** restore action-capable tapered arms, load-bearing legs, and broad brown-gray extremities; recut clothing around them.
- **Tall human in costume:** shorten the total body, broaden the belly and shoulder transition, remove the waist and narrow pelvis, shorten both thigh and shin, and redrape clothing around the corrected mass.
- **Long cylindrical robe:** reconstruct the pear-shaped body underneath, widen the middle and lower torso, raise or compress the hem, and make the garment describe the belly rather than conceal it.
- **Human-size clothing:** rebuild the body first, then resize collar, shoulders, sleeves, torso panels, belt, hem, legwear, gloves, footwear, armor, and headwear. Preserve design details while moving every seam and attachment to the Nailong anatomy.
- **Stretched texture costume:** redraw patterns, fasteners, borders, and embroidery across the new panels with believable spacing; do not scale or warp the whole human garment as one flat layer.
- **Invented exposed belly:** restore every source garment panel and its original closure state; cover the cream patch completely wherever the source torso was covered, then express the large abdomen only through external cloth volume and folds.
- **Altered garment opening:** restore the original neckline, lapel overlap, buttons, ties, fasteners, slit, hem, and opacity. Do not use a new opening as a character-recognition shortcut.
- **Mounted human proportions:** greatly enlarge the abdomen over the saddle, remove the human waist and pelvis, simplify the leg anatomy, restore a large belly patch, and reconnect boots or feet to the stirrups with the minimum natural reach required by the pose.
- **Missing/floating headwear:** restore original hair and headwear, fit them to the crown, and preserve visible identity anchors.
- **Style mismatch:** restore the source palette, material, grain, aging, edge softness, and spatial detail level rather than adding more character detail.

## Final Silhouette Test

Before delivery, temporarily judge each Nailong without relying on facial features, yellow color, or costume:

1. At thumbnail size, the figure must read as a wide, low-centered, pear/bean-shaped Nailong rather than a standing human.
2. The belly must remain the dominant mass even under a robe, cape, uniform, or seated pose.
3. Limbs must remain visually subordinate to the huge abdomen, use smooth tapered Nailong anatomy, and still complete the action and maintain every functional contact.
4. Source and result must expose the same body regions through the clothing. A newly visible cream patch or yellow torso is an automatic failure.
5. If covering the face makes the result read as a human body, the conversion fails. Rebuild the clothed mass and pose—without opening the garment—before polishing texture.
