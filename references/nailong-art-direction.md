# Nailong Artwork Integration Guide

## Core Shape

The result should look as if the original artist painted Nailong into the work. It must not resemble a sticker, mascot suit, toy, or generic dragon.

- Only the crown is rounded; the head is vertically elongated, not a sphere.
- Face, back of head, sloping shoulders, and torso form one continuous silhouette with no jawline or neck.
- The body widens into a soft pear/bean/water-drop form with a large round belly.
- Green circular eyes sit against the face curvature with black pupils matching the source gaze; no eye stalks or protrusion.
- The face has no human nose bridge, ears, chin, or brow ridge.
- The small mouth needs a readable opening, upper/lower edge, and dark inward depth; never a flat line, beak, tube, dolphin snout, or muzzle.
- Warm-yellow hairless body; pale-cream oval belly patch adapted to light and clothing.

## Limbs

Limbs are short relative to the large belly, but never tiny stubs. Arms emerge broadly from sloping shoulders and have readable upper-arm and forearm length for hanging, crossing the belly, raising overhead, embracing, or holding props. Legs extend clearly below the belly with enough length and spacing to bear weight; seated poses retain a readable thigh-to-lower-leg bend. Hands and feet are broad, blunt, muted brown-gray mitten/paddle forms, with only simplified soft digits when gripping requires them.

## Hair and Headwear

Preserve the original hairstyle, hair silhouette, bun, hat, crown, headscarf, flowers, pins, and identity-bearing ornaments. Refit them to the elongated rounded crown with believable contact and occlusion. Do not default to a bald Nailong. Headwear may cover part of the crown but must not hide the core green eyes, small mouth, or continuous no-neck silhouette.

## Clothing, Props, and Groups

Recut collars, hats, sleeves, armor, belts, jewelry, and shoes around the round crown, belly, and substantial limbs. Preserve prop placement, direction, function, and physical contact. In groups, retain figure count, hierarchy, gaze network, scale, and occlusion; distinguish characters through their original clothing, pose, body scale, role, and expression rather than cloning one model.

## Medium Adaptation

- **Classical oil:** match glazing, transparent shadows, ground color, canvas, aging, and edge hierarchy.
- **Impressionism/expressionism:** integrate the silhouette into the source brush rhythm; eyes and mouth must not become unnaturally crisp.
- **Traditional painting/woodblock:** translate identity into controlled line, flat mineral color, ink, carved marks, paper or silk texture.
- **Cubism/modernism:** reconstruct the crown, green eyes, mouth, yellow torso, belly patch, and limbs through the source’s planes, displaced viewpoints, and geometric simplification; never make a smooth 3D toy.
- **Photography:** match lens, depth, noise, reflection, color temperature, and contact shadow without becoming a person in a costume.

## Review and Repairs

At normal size, confirm that every target reads instantly as Nailong and still belongs to the artwork.

- **Human yellow face:** remove nose, ears, jaw, neck, and realistic fingers; restore the continuous crown-to-shoulder silhouette.
- **Round ball head:** elongate the head and flow its sides into sloping shoulders.
- **Eye stalks:** embed the eyes into the face curvature.
- **Line mouth or snout:** add inward mouth depth and flatten any projecting muzzle.
- **Traditional dragon traits:** remove horns, wings, scales, fangs, claws, spines, and long tail.
- **Stub limbs or hidden body:** restore substantial arms, load-bearing legs, and broad brown-gray extremities; recut clothing around them.
- **Missing/floating headwear:** restore original hair and headwear, fit them to the crown, and preserve visible identity anchors.
- **Style mismatch:** restore the source palette, material, grain, aging, edge softness, and spatial detail level rather than adding more character detail.
